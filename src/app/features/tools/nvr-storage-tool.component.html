Storage = bitrate ÷ 8 × seconds per day × days × cameras × share of the day recording, in decimal units. This is the formula in Genetec's documentation (500 Kbps for 7 days = 37.8 GB). The bitrate is the one number that decides the answer, and it changes with the scene: motion, lighting, night noise and compression settings all move it (Axis, "Bitrate control for IP video"). Use a real bitrate whenever you can.

Estimates use IC Realtime's published bitrate ranges for H.264 (5 to 30 fps: 720p 384 to 2,048 Kbps; 2MP and 4MP and 5MP 768 to 4,096; 8MP/4K 1,536 to 8,192; 12MP 2,560 to 12,288. 60 fps: 4,096, 8,192, 8,192, 8,192, 16,400 and 18,432 Kbps). H.265 uses about half, per the same guide. The result is a low to high range and the high end is the one to quote.

Drives: 1 TB = one trillion bytes (Seagate). RAID 5 loses one drive to parity and RAID 6 loses two (QNAP); RAID 10 keeps half the total and needs an even number of drives, at least four (Dell). RAID 5 needs at least three drives and RAID 6 at least four. NVRs also reserve a little space, so leave some headroom.
